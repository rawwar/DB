# Practice And Progression

Learning should advance through demonstrated ability, not through exposure. The learner should practise each concept before moving on.

## Practice Types

Use a mix of these practice modes.

### Recall

The learner explains the concept without looking back.

Good prompts:

- "Explain this in your own words."
- "What problem does this solve?"
- "What are the two most important rules?"

### Prediction

The learner predicts behavior before seeing the answer.

Good prompts:

- "What will this print?"
- "Which branch runs?"
- "What error do you expect?"
- "What changes if this line moves?"

### Modification

The learner changes a working example.

Good prompts:

- "Change this so it handles one more case."
- "Rename this piece and explain what else must change."
- "Make the example fail, then fix it."

### Construction

The learner builds a small example from scratch.

Good prompts:

- "Write the smallest version yourself."
- "Implement the same idea with different names."
- "Use this concept in a new tiny problem."

### Debugging

The learner diagnoses a broken example.

Good prompts:

- "Find the bug."
- "Explain the wrong assumption."
- "Fix it with the smallest change."

## Difficulty Ladder

Move through difficulty in this order:

1. Recognize the concept.
2. Explain the concept.
3. Trace a worked example.
4. Modify a worked example.
5. Build a small example.
6. Debug a broken example.
7. Use the concept with one earlier concept.

Do not skip directly to large projects.

## Mastery Gates

Before proceeding, the learner should pass a small check.

A concept is ready enough when the learner can:

- Define it simply.
- Use it in a small example.
- Explain why the example works.
- Make one small change correctly.
- Avoid or fix the most common beginner mistake.

If the learner fails the check:

- Do not proceed.
- Identify the missing sub-skill.
- Give a smaller exercise.
- Re-test with a different but similar prompt.

## Spaced Review

Future lessons should briefly reuse older ideas.

Use review naturally:

- Start a lesson with one quick recall question.
- Include one old concept inside a new exercise.
- Ask the learner to compare the new idea with a previous idea.

Do not turn review into a long detour unless the learner is blocked.

## Exercise Design Rules

Exercises should be:

- Small enough to complete soon.
- Specific enough to know when done.
- Focused on the current concept.
- Slightly varied from the example.
- Reviewable by the LLM.

Avoid exercises that require several untaught ideas at once.
